## Independent derivation of relaxed optimum (analytic; not Lean)
Symmetrize any real trigonometric majorant g under k -> -k, preserving mean cost. Let N=r+1, q=Dg-1>=0. Then q is real even of degree <=N and its Laurent continuation has q(t)=-1 at t=exp(kappa)>1. Real Fejer-Riesz gives q(z)=p(z)p(1/z) with real p of degree <=N. Conversely a nonnegative even Laurent polynomial q of degree <=N with q(t)=-1 has q+1 divisible by D in the Laurent ring: multiplying by z^N gives a polynomial with roots t and 1/t. The quotient g has degree <=N-1 and majorizes 1/D. Write a for p coefficients, u_j=t^j, v_j=t^-j for j=0,...,N. The constraint is a^T (uv^T+vu^T)/2 a=-1; the cost is ||a||^2. The negative eigenvalue is (u.v-||u||||v||)/2, attained by u/||u||-v/||v||. Here u.v=N+1 and ||u||||v||=sum_{j=0}^N t^(2j-N)=sinh((N+1)kappa)/sinh(kappa). Scaling the negative eigenvector gives equality. Hence Eq (14) is the exact relaxed optimum for this lattice dispersion. At fixed m>0, L_r ~ 4 sinh(kappa) exp(-(r+2)kappa). Since every admissible FIR family supplies a relaxed majorant, the ratio of the physical FIR infimum to this relaxation is Theta(exp(kappa*r)/r^2), using the already established two-sided FIR bounds. This is an analytic corollary, not machine verified. It is not a claim about the full Golden Rule class.

## Matrix moment theory: sharpened theorem-level comparison
