# Analytic proof checkpoint (not publication-ready)

For real amplitudes a_v(j), j=0,...,r, set P_v(z)=sum_j a_v(j) z^j. On the unit circle the coefficient of z^d in |P_v|^2 is sum_j a_v(j)a_v(j+d) for d>=0, since conjugate(z)=z^-1. Orthogonality under normalized dk/(2*pi) extracts each coefficient. Hence sum |P|^2=1 and sum T|P|^2=c-z-z^-1 imply the four identities used in Core.lean, with the index shift a_Core(j+1)=a(j) and zero padding. Multiplying P by (T-c)z+z^2+1 gives exactly the padded residual coefficients indexed 0,...,r+2. Parseval gives H=average sum |P|^2(T-D)^2/T. Entropy.lean gives H=average D sigma <=(c+2) average sigma, since sigma>=0. Thus sigma_average>=1/(c(c+2)r^2).

For the upper bound take n=r+1, x=pi/(n+1), g_j=sqrt(2/(n+1)) sin((j+1)x). The geometric sum of exp(2ijx), j=0,...,n, vanishes; taking real parts proves sum g_j^2=1. With g_-1=g_n=0 the sine addition law gives g_(j-1)+g_(j+1)=2 cos(x)g_j. Multiplying by g_j and summing gives rho=sum g_j g_(j+1)=cos(x)>0 (r>=1). Take N=2r+5, theta_l=2pi l/N, two real channels g_j cos(j theta_l)/sqrt(N), g_j sin(j theta_l)/sqrt(N), and common T_l=c-2cos(theta_l)/rho.

Write C_d=sum_j g_j g_(j+d) for 0<=d<=r. The combined channel weight is
w_l(k)=(1+2 sum_{d=1}^r C_d cos(d theta_l) cos(d k))/N.
Indeed the double sum of real products has factor cos((i-j)theta_l); pairing i-j=d with -d yields the displayed expansion. The finite geometric series gives average_l cos(d theta_l)=0 for 1<=d<=r, and average_l cos(theta_l)cos(d theta_l)=delta_{d,1}/2 because N>r+1. Thus sum_l w_l=1, sum_l T_l w_l=c-2 cos(k), identically for every k. Each w_l is the sum of squared moduli of two actual real amplitude polynomials, not an arbitrary signed trigonometric polynomial.

Average_k w_l=1/N, so average_k sum_l T_l^2 w_l=c^2+2/rho^2 by discrete orthogonality at harmonics 1 and 2. Average_k D^2=c^2+2. Variance_identity yields average_k sum_l w_l(T_l-D)^2=2(rho^-2-1). Entropy_upper therefore gives sigma_average<=2 tan(x)^2/[m(c-2/rho)] when c=m+2 and c>2/rho. If r+2>=pi sqrt((m+4)/m), cos(x)>=1-x^2/2>= (m+8)/(2(m+4)) >=4/(m+4). Consequently T_l>=m/2 and tan(x)^2<=x^2 (m+4)^2/16. This proves the claimed upper bound pi^2(m+4)^2/[4m^2(r+2)^2]. (The first inequality for rho can also be weakened directly to 4/(m+4).) Combined with the lower bound this gives order r^-2 for fixed m>0. For small r>=1, admissibility is nonempty by the two baths (1 +/- z)/2 with temperatures c-/+2; r=0 cannot realize the nonconstant D. The constants are not uniform as m tends to zero.
