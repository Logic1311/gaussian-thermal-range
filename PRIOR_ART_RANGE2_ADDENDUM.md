# Priority and applicability reassessment — exact range-two extension

Date: 2026-11-26. Internal AI-assisted review; not an independent referee report. The previous PRIOR_ART.md and SDP addendum remain part of the record.

## Closest physical problem

S. Gopalakrishnan, *Thermodynamic cost of locally maintaining a steady state*, arXiv:2610.38925v1, submitted 2026-10-28, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38925; accessible indexed mirror https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.38925. Latest records located during this review still identify the submission as v1; no final journal publication or later version was verified. The SciPost submission header is not evidence of acceptance. Checked the indexed text of equations (10)–(18) and the Figure 2 caption. Equation (10) is the channel-weighted Clausius expression. Equation (11) concerns Markov length, a different resource constraint. Equation (12) deliberately relaxes the bath decomposition; (14) gives its scalar majorant optimum, (15) the large-Markov-length coefficient at fixed range, and (16)–(18) the nearest-neighbor construction. The range-two coefficient 1/5 in the small-mass leading asymptotics follows from the known scalar formula. The new claims are physical saturation in that limit and the full positive-mass range-two minimum and its exact three-temperature realization, not the original scalar coefficient or relaxation. The strict gap excludes every feasible FIR realization approaching the relaxed optimum at fixed m>0, r>=2. The new degree-two exact formula gives a more decisive physical prediction than upper/lower scaling bounds.

The original Golden Rule rates involve both an interaction factor and momentum-dependent density of states. Finite support of the interaction factor alone is insufficient to force polynomial individual rates with unrestricted density of states. The paper's two normalizations and individual-rate degree restriction define the exact class under study. Evenization and real Fejer–Riesz factorization identify the attainable average costs for that degree-bounded nonnegative trigonometric-rate class. They do not prove an equivalence for arbitrary microscopic DOS. This restriction is an essential hypothesis, not a technical footnote.

## Matrix moment theory: sharpened theorem-level comparison

V. M. Adamyan and I. M. Tkachenko, *Solution of the Stieltjes truncated matrix moment problem*, Opuscula Mathematica 25 (2005), 5–24, DOI 10.7494/OpMath.2005.25.1.5. Accessible full text: https://www.opuscula.agh.edu.pl/vol25/1/art/opuscula_math_2501.pdf. Theorem 2.1 concerns Hermitian moments C_0,...,C_{2n} represented by a nonnegative matrix measure on [0,infinity). It requires a positive Hankel matrix, a shifted positive Hankel matrix, and the associated nullspace conditions (their (5)–(7)). At n=1, with (C_0,C_1,C_2)=(J,G,H), these specialize to block positivity, G>=0, ker J subset ker H, and ker G subset ker H. Their support includes zero. By contrast, a physical triple has identical kernels of G,H,J and support strictly inside (0,infinity). The block SDP by itself is only the closed optimization cone: its singular triples need not satisfy the additional nullspace conditions. Reweighting the bath matrix measure by 1/T explains the exact ordinary Stieltjes relationship. Positive definite G,H and Schur-saturated J admit a positive-temperature spectral realization; mixing the strictly positive reference restores the interior for any SDP feasible point. The new optimization-specific lag constraints, trace objective, finite positive-temperature reconstruction at its range-two optimum and explicit quartic dual certificate are not the statements of Theorem 2.1. The theorem nevertheless supplies an established general moment-theoretic route and cannot be claimed as a new mathematical foundation here.

A. Zalar and I. Zobovic, *The Matricial Univariate Rational Truncated Moment Problem*, arXiv:2608.28227 (submitted 2026-08-28), https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28227. This round obtained the indexed theorem text (1340 lines; some displayed equations not exposed). Theorem 1.1 treats a fully specified linear map on rational functions f/q, with bounded numerator degree and a fixed denominator with even-order real zeros. It reduces existence to the associated polynomial moments on R or [0,infinity), with positivity, column-range/localizing constraints and avoidance of each pole via the shifted-column sets A^(r). It also characterizes a minimal total atomic multiplicity equal to the moment matrix rank. Theorem 5.1 starts with an already representable polynomial Stieltjes mapping and proves simultaneous minimal multiplicities at finitely many distinct prescribed nonnegative nodes for one rank-minimal representing measure. The proof uses the same flat extension while choosing the extra moment sufficiently positive for the Stieltjes localizing inequality; its input is not an arbitrary PSD block. For our data 1/T,1,T, an even denominator T^2 would place these among T/T^2,T^2/T^2,T^3/T^2, leaving additional moments unspecified. The full rational problem is not directly supplied by just our three matrices; the reweighted ordinary degree-two Stieltjes problem is more economical. Pole avoidance and finite atomic existence are established general mathematical mechanisms. Neither theorem imposes the spatial lag constraints of G and H or minimizes the Fourier entropy functional. In particular neither supplies the quartic, its root bracket, or attainment of the constrained optimal physical triple. The new contribution is this specific exact optimization, not general rational moment representability or the notion of minimal atomic multiplicity. See the author's current publication list at https://hugo.fri.uni-lj.si/sl/o-fakulteti/osebje/aljaz-zalar-0, which lists this work as submitted. Direct PDF retrieval failed, and indexed formula omissions limit the audit; no assertion is made that every displayed condition was independently read in the PDF.

S. Boyd and L. Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization* (2004), Example 3.4, Sec. 5.2.3, https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/; and J. V. Burke, Y. Gao, T. Hoheisel, *Variational properties of matrix functions via the generalized matrix-fractional function*, SIAM J. Optim. 29 (2019), 1958–1987, DOI 10.1137/18M1209660. Matrix-fractional convexity, Schur complement and convex epigraph closure are established methods. The exact value problem is a special constrained application, and our direct Toeplitz multiplier certificate avoids needing a general new duality theorem. The two-temperature minimum, critical saturation, and joint range/palette restrictions concern the physical channel class, not those general optimization statements.

## Nearby new literature and negative comparisons

A. Jindal and A. Zalar, *Operator Fejer–Riesz Theorems with Gaps and Degree Bounds*, arXiv:2611.02967v1 (2026-11-02), https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02967. Abstract/metadata and mirror introduction available. This concerns certificate degrees for positive operator-valued polynomials on compact semialgebraic subsets of the real line, including interval unions, and uses a symmetric matrix pencil. It does not solve simultaneous FIR bath normalizations and the reciprocal-temperature cost. Treat as a relevant lead for other support models, not as fully audited priority evidence.

D. Tupkary, A. Dhar, M. Kulkarni, A. Purkayastha, *Searching for Lindbladians obeying local conservation laws and showing thermalization*, arXiv:2301.02146 (2023), https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02146. Abstract-level comparison: SDP feasibility for local quantum Markov generators with a fixed-temperature thermalization criterion and conservation laws, not the classical scalar Gaussian multi-temperature entropy optimum. It illustrates that using SDPs in thermalization is not new. No theorem-level equivalence asserted.

Previous Kopotun et al. positive-approximation and Pages–Wilbertz / Jourdain–Pages quantization comparisons remain binding: joint r^-2 + K^-2 order and classical factorization/compression tools are supporting results. Quantization assumes scalar node mixing, not polynomial weights that simultaneously normalize every continuous Fourier mode at finite degree. The exact finite-range gap and small-mass physical saturation go beyond those particular bounds; the new degree-two optimum additionally supplies an attained finite-parameter value. Neither theorem nor an abstract numerical optimization result establishes the novelty or broad physical significance of this exact specialization.

## Search limits and disposition

Targeted searches for the original title, related citations, finite-range multi-temperature Gaussian entropy minimization, quartic optimization, Stieltjes matrix moments, and operator Fejer–Riesz degree constraints did not identify a previously published identical all-mass range-two formula or a stronger directly covering thermal theorem. This is a negative search observation, not a priority proof. Primary arXiv/PDF access and screenshot requests were intermittently unavailable, including screenshots of the Gopalakrishnan and Adamyan–Tkachenko PDFs. Indexed text and exact theorem statements were used where accessible, with missing displays explicitly noted above. Complete backward/forward citation coverage and independent expert judgments remain unverified.

Decision: strongest review claims are (i) finite-range strict physical gap, (ii) sharp physical critical realization, (iii) the new exact range-two attained minimum and minimum temperature count. The general SDP is a useful computational and analytical bridge whose mathematical foundations are established. Do not infer A grade from these facts alone. Invite a statistical physicist and a matrix-moment/convex-optimization specialist, privately and only after explicit authorization, to attempt falsification and assess significance. No communication or release has occurred.
